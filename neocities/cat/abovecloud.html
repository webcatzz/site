adrift upon a vapor ocean...

the thin strip of abovecloud civilization, informally known as the layer, is populated by a shifting network of floating cities, among a host of other animals.

cities

in the psychology of the meowdern civilized aerite, it is difficult to overstate the significance of the city.

cities are the beating heart of human activity. earth civilization can spread near-infinitely onto neighboring ground, but on aerie, there is nothing beyond the city walls (so to speak). in a choice between an existing city or casting their lot to the empty air, most choose the city.

cities also serve an important cultural role: on earth, people most commonly identify themselves with their home country, whereas aerie does not have countries—cities drift around too much to form geographical relationships. instead, each city is its own city-state—generally governed by a mayor or city council—and it is cities that take the primary role as a cultural identifier. think if the greek ideals of a city never went away.

city taxonomy

the primary goal of a city is to stay afloat. there are couple of different approaches, with some overlap:

  • platform cities, the most common variety, are effectively large foundations that support themselves with a handful of well-maintained power sources.
  • symbiote cities hook themselves onto large docile animals. no matter how long-lived the animal, though, the animal eventually grows old and necessitates a move.
  • flotsam cities decentralize themselves into many separate structures, either using individual power sources or contributing to and drawing from a shared power source.

much of the character of a city comes from where it lands in these categories and, if applicable, the method it uses for generating power.

one thing they all share in common, though, is that they feel rather like ocean vessels: everything is bolted down and sprinkled with lights, since they have to be built to withstand both extreme weather and extreme darkness. for reasons discussed above, they also tend to become pretty cramped.

architecturally, they tend to emulate the ruins they drift over, albeit without any of their original context—most large cities become a towering patchwork of different styles from a variety of places and periods.
